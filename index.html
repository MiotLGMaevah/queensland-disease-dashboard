02 / Introduction

What do these reports tell us?

Explore volume and composition, with the context needed to interpret both.

The purpose

This dashboard describes which conditions were reported most often from 2010 to 2014, compares STI-related reports with other conditions, and groups the full dataset into similar disease families.

It is a descriptive view of reported counts. It does not measure individual risk, disease severity, treatment outcomes, or unique patients.

The dataset

The dataset supplied via Kaggle covers Queensland, Australia, from January 2010 to December 2014. The supplied CSV began with 1,033 records and four fields: year, quarter, condition, and counts. It contains 67 condition labels across 20 quarters.

After adding 307 assumed-zero combinations, it contains 1,340 rows. Labels were standardized, reporting status was retained, and each condition received one analytical category.

How to explore

Hover over a bar to see its exact count and share of all reports. Click or tap a bar to open the selected condition or category, including annual counts and its contributing conditions.

Use “Explore this chart” for the broader explanation behind each view. Return to the dashboard whenever you want to compare another group.

Read the counts carefully

Syphilis (Total) equals its three subcategories in every quarter. Only the total contributes to chart totals. Absent records were treated as assumed zeros, not verified zero-case reports.

No minimum count cutoff is applied. Categories are practical, mutually exclusive analytical groups, not an official diagnostic classification. Hepatitis B remains in hepatitis; exposure and vaccination-event reports remain separate.

The corresponding official dataset describes cases of communicable diseases in Queensland notified to the Department of Health, grouped by disease and quarter, January 2010–December 2014.

Open official dataset ↗

The official portal lists Creative Commons Attribution 3.0. This dashboard is an adapted analysis and is not endorsed by Queensland Health or Kaggle.

Adaptations by Maevah Miot

Condition labels and punctuation were standardized. The 307 absent condition–quarter combinations were assigned assumed zeros, with a reporting-status column identifying them. All original numeric counts were preserved.

Conditions were assigned to 13 analytical categories. Syphilis subcategories are excluded from chart totals to prevent double-counting; the total is retained. The category mapping is specific to this dashboard.

Scope and interpretation

Geography: Queensland, Australia. Reporting period: 2010–2014. Counts describe reports, not unique patients, population-adjusted rates, or verified transmission routes.

Source reference checked October 3, 2026. The official portal identifies a corresponding original dataset; the Kaggle page could not be independently inspected. No minimum count threshold is applied.
